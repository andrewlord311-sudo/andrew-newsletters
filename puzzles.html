Tough Logic Masters Deutschland

The Main Hall — the free Sudoku Mansion showcase

by Nurator — posted to the Rätselportal to mark Sudoku Mansion's release on 19 August 2026
Normal sudoku rules, plus four constraint families stacked on one grid. Quadruples: every digit shown in a small circle at a four-cell intersection must appear somewhere in those four surrounding cells. Kropki pairs: a black dot between two cells means one digit is exactly double the other; a white dot means they're consecutive — and crucially not all dots are given, so an absent dot is information too. German whispers: adjacent digits along a green line differ by at least 5, which means no 5 can ever sit on a whisper and the line has to alternate low (1–4) and high (6–9). Region sum lines: wherever a line crosses a box border, each segment inside a box sums to the same total. The puzzle page carries the setter's exact wording and a play link.
Why you won't have seen it: Sudoku Mansion is a story-driven game of 50-plus handcrafted variant sudoku by a setter who works through the German portal, not the YouTube circuit. This grid is the free sample of its core mechanic.

Twisted Whispers

Rätselportal, published 22 August 2026 — setter credited on the puzzle page
Normal sudoku rules, plus two line types that pull in opposite directions. A German whispers line forces consecutive cells along it to differ by at least 5 — maximum spread. A palindrome line reads the same forwards and backwards, so the digit in the first cell equals the digit in the last, the second equals the second-to-last, and so on — maximum repetition. The interest is in what happens where the two interact: a palindrome copies digits into positions a whisper is simultaneously trying to force apart, and pinning down which of the low/high alternations survives is most of the solve.
Why you won't have seen it: six days old, posted straight to a German-language portal — this is the kind of puzzle that gets a CTC video eighteen months later, if ever.

Gentle A new ruleset being trialled

AUGUST (2026 Version)

Rätselportal — the setter's deliberately easy test-drive of a rule they've just invented
Normal sudoku rules, plus a coloured-dot constraint the setter has devised themselves — the definition is on the puzzle page, since it isn't a standard variant you'll know from anywhere else. They've pitched this grid soft on purpose: solvers describe it as very smooth and easy, a perfect Sunday-morning snack. The point isn't the difficulty, it's that the setter is openly asking whether the ruleset is worth developing, and says they'll write harder sudoku with it if the reception is good.
Why you won't have seen it: it's a brand-new constraint with no name yet and no video, being play-tested in public. Getting in at this stage is the opposite of watching a recap.

🌍 The wider puzzle worldA trading firm's monthly problem, and the hardest pencil puzzles published anywhere

Monster Jane Street

Andy's Afternoon Amble — August's puzzle, open until month end

Jane Street's puzzle programme — one problem a month, published since 2015
The trading firm posts a single puzzle each month and takes submissions until the month closes, at which point the puzzle and a full worked solution move into a public archive going back a decade. They are not quick teasers: the typical Jane Street puzzle is a combinatorial or geometric optimisation that rewards writing a bit of code as readily as it rewards a clean insight, and the archive is the real prize — dozens of self-contained problems, each with its solution, free to work through in any order. This month's is Andy's Afternoon Amble, and August ends on Monday, so there's a weekend's window to have a go while it's still live.
Why you won't have seen it: it circulates in quant and competitive-programming circles, not puzzle-app ones, and each puzzle is only "current" for a few weeks.

Monster Grandmaster Puzzles

The Sunday Stumper series — deliberately brutal, with the logic shown

Grandmaster Puzzles (The Art of Puzzles), Thomas Snyder's publishing house — roughly monthly since 2021
Not sudoku at all: pencil-puzzle genres, set to championship standard. The Sunday Stumper is the house's hardest slot — about one a month, each with a guaranteed logical solving path (no guessing) and, importantly, a solution video walking through how that path is found. That last part is what makes them worth attempting rather than bouncing off. Two genres to start with: Fillomino, where you divide the grid into polyomino regions such that each number gives the area of the region containing it, and no two regions of equal size may touch edge-to-edge; and Yajilin, a loop genre where arrow clues count shaded cells in a direction and you must draw one closed loop through every unshaded cell.
Why you won't have seen it: GM Puzzles is a subscription-and-blog operation with no algorithmic feed — it's the deep end of the pencil-puzzle world, and it never crosses into the daily-app ecosystem.

➗ Maths puzzle of the weekFiddler on the Proof — the direct successor to FiveThirtyEight's Riddler

"How Far Can You Roll?" — The Fiddler, 21 August 2026
Frederica Fiddleria is setting up a photography show. She attaches a light to a point on the circumference of a circular wheel of radius 1 metre, and rolls the wheel along the ground through exactly one complete revolution. How long is the path the light traces out?
A lovely one because the wrong answers are so tempting — it is not 2π, and it is not the wheel's diameter times anything obvious. The light is doing two things at once: going round and going along. Pencil, paper and a calculator will get you there, and there's an Extra Credit variant underneath for when it does. Zach Wissner-Gross posts a fresh problem every Friday morning and publishes reader solutions the following week, so nothing is spoiled by reading now.
