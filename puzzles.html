Medium Wed 5 Aug

"Dorlir's unsquished sudoku"

by Jonesy
Normal sudoku rules apply. A digit in a circle shows how many times that digit appears among circles; each circle is the centre of a 3×3 area containing 9 unique digits (edge circles only reach 6 cells). Digits joined by an X sum to 10. One tidy extra layer on top of standard sudoku — a good one to warm up on.

Tough Thu 6 Aug

"Anti & Pro Knights"

by Eric Bader
Normal sudoku rules apply, plus cages summing to their given total, green lines where adjacent digits differ by at least 5, and pink lines holding a consecutive run of digits in any order. No two 9s may be a knight's move apart; every 1 must be a knight's move from at least two other 1s — and knight's moves wrap around the grid cylindrically, like a torus. Fiddly to keep straight, satisfying once it clicks.

Monster Thu 6 Aug

"Consecutive Regional Constraints"

by Belamis
The grid starts hidden under dynamic fog — correct placements reveal more of it. Seven line/region types are in play (arrow, region sum, renban, Dutch whisper, entropic, Kropki, parity), no constraint type may repeat a digit across the whole grid, and the digits used within each type must themselves form one consecutive run. A proper sit-down-with-a-coffee puzzle, not a quick one.

💼 LinkedIn games worth a coffee breakThese reset daily, so head to the hub and play today's — here's what's worth prioritising

🔗 Zip
Draw a single path through every numbered checkpoint in order without crossing yourself. Simple rules, but the grid shapes get genuinely fiddly toward the end of the week — worth doing daily rather than catching up in one go.
🔢 Mini Sudoku
A stripped-down 5×5 (or similar small-grid) sudoku — the classic game, just quick enough for a coffee break. Good palate cleanser if the CTC puzzles above have you cross-eyed.
🧩 Patches
Currently on the LinkedIn line-up: divide the grid into non-overlapping rectangles so every numbered cell anchors a shape of exactly that many cells, with no gaps or overlaps left over. Plays like a tidy dissection puzzle — start from the prime-numbered clues (2, 3, 5, 7), they only fit one way.

"Level-Headed" — Boris Kordemsky, The Moscow Puzzles (1972)
"A house has 6 storeys, each the same height. How many times as long is the ascent to the sixth floor as the ascent to the third?"
It isn't 2. Worth thirty seconds of thought before you check — the trick is in what "ascent to a floor" actually counts.
