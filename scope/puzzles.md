# Puzzle Weekly — scope

**This file defines what the newsletter covers.** The weekly task reads it at the start of
every run and treats it as authoritative. To change the newsletter, edit this file — you do
not need to touch the scheduled task's prompt.

## Subject

A weekly roundup of Andrew's favourite kinds of puzzle, timed to land Friday morning so there's
a stack of them for the weekend. **Word puzzles (crosswords, anagrams, word searches, Wordle-
likes) are explicitly out of scope** — Andrew isn't a fan, don't include them even if they're
popular or easy to source.

## Guiding principle — explore, don't recap (added 2026-08-08, reader request)

Andrew named Cracking the Cryptic, LinkedIn Games, Thinky Games and his Facebook maths feed as
**examples of the kind of puzzle he likes** — CTC-style sudoku with extra constraint layers,
slick daily logic games, maths/logic puzzles. He already follows all of those himself. **The
newsletter's whole job is to surface things he hasn't already seen**, not to recap the same
sites' own weekly lineups back at him. Concretely:

- Every issue must include at least 2 puzzles from a source **not** in the "named examples"
  list above — see "Sources to explore" below for where to look.
- When picking sudoku variants, don't default to "whatever CTC posted on YouTube this week" —
  that's exactly the recap problem. Vary the *setter* and *platform*, not just the puzzle.
- It's fine — good, even — for an issue to have zero LinkedIn or Thinky Games picks some weeks
  if better material turned up elsewhere. They're not a mandatory quota, just one option.
- A short one-line "why this is new" framing on each pick (e.g. "a Logic Masters India setter
  you won't have seen on CTC's channel", "this genre doesn't exist on LinkedIn's daily lineup")
  helps confirm each week that the brief is being honoured, not just checked off.

## Sections to cover

1. **Sudoku variants** (the main event) — 2–3 puzzles with extra constraint layers on top of
   standard sudoku: killer, thermo, arrow, sandwich, XV, renban, German whispers, dynamic fog,
   or anything else in that family. **Actively rotate setters and platforms** rather than
   always pulling this week's CTC video roster — see "Sources to explore" below. Every puzzle
   needs a real, working "play it" link (sudokupad.app links are ideal — playable in-browser,
   no app required) and a one-line plain-English explanation of its special rule(s). Note the
   setter/source and roughly how hard it is if that's discoverable.
2. **Wider puzzle world** (the actual point of this newsletter) — 2+ puzzles/games from
   somewhere in "Sources to explore" below that Andrew is unlikely to have already seen this
   week. Can be logic-grid puzzles, variant pencil puzzles (Fillomino, Nurikabe, Slitherlink,
   Kakuro...), a puzzle-hunt-style challenge, a competition archive pull, or anything else that
   fits his taste (constraint logic / maths, not word puzzles) but isn't from his named sites.
3. **LinkedIn daily games and/or Thinky Games dailies** — optional, not mandatory. Include only
   if something's genuinely worth a shout that week (e.g. an unusually tricky Queens, a new
   Thinky title). Reset daily, so don't try to link one exact day's puzzle — link to
   https://www.linkedin.com/games/ or https://www.thinkygames.com/daily-puzzles and say why.
4. **Maths puzzle of the week** — Andrew likes the maths puzzles that turn up on his Facebook
   feed, but that feed isn't something Claude can read. Substitute one genuinely good puzzle —
   ideally rotating sources (see "Sources to explore"), not the same one or two every week.
   Give the puzzle itself (or a clear teaser) plus a link to work through/reveal the answer,
   without spoiling it in the newsletter text.

## Sources to explore (rotate through these, don't settle on favourites)

- **Logic Masters India** (logic-masters-india.com) and **Logic Masters Germany** / Rätselportal
  — run frequent online variant-sudoku and logic-puzzle competitions with high-quality setters
  Andrew won't have encountered via CTC's own channel.
- **GM Puzzles** (gmpuzzles.com) — Grandmaster-level variant logic puzzles beyond sudoku
  (Fillomino, Slitherlink, Nurikabe, Kakuro, Skyscrapers, etc.), same "extra constraint" DNA.
- **World Puzzle Federation** (wpf.st) competition/archive puzzles — Sudoku GP, WSC/WPC
  archives, genuinely championship-tier material.
- **f-puzzles.com** and **sudokupad.app**'s own community/setter listings, not just the puzzles
  that happen to get a CTC video — huge numbers of constructors post there directly.
- **Alex Bellos' puzzle column** (The Guardian, "Alex Bellos's Monday puzzle") — maths/logic
  puzzles from a completely different lineage to Presh Talwalkar/Brilliant.
- **Jane Street puzzles**, **Two Sigma puzzles**, **MIT Mystery Hunt** archive puzzles, **Puzzling
  StackExchange**'s highly-voted weekly puzzles — sharper, more unusual maths/logic puzzles
  than typical listicle sources.
- **Nikoli** (nikoli.co.jp/en) — the original Japanese puzzle publisher (they invented Sudoku's
  modern form and many pencil-puzzle genres) — a great "you've never seen this" well.
- **r/sudoku** and **r/puzzles** puzzle-showcase threads — individual constructors sharing new
  work, often well before/without a CTC video.
- Reuse the same source only occasionally — if last week pulled from GM Puzzles, this week
  should probably look elsewhere first.

## Rating — how it feeds back in

Each puzzle/section has 👍 / 🙂 / 👎 one-click links that log straight to a Google Sheet (no
page to fill in). Before writing each week's issue, fetch the ratings summary (URL in the
scheduled task prompt) and use it to shift emphasis for **next** week — more of what's rated
well, less/none of what isn't, by category/style/difficulty (e.g. "killer sudokus keep getting
👍, thermo doesn't — lean killer/arrow"). It can't fetch you the literal same puzzle again since
these are one-off dailies; it's steering the *kind* of puzzle picked, not a repeat.
