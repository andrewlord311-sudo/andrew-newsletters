🕊️ BACP remembers Dame Esther Rantzen

Dame Esther Rantzen, the broadcaster and children's-welfare campaigner, died on 30 September aged 86, after a long illness. BACP's own tribute remembers her as more than a broadcaster who covered the counselling world from the outside: she was a trained volunteer counsellor and a former BACP Vice-President, and was a keynote speaker alongside then-BACP Chair Dr Andrew Reeves at a 2017 conference on children's and young people's mental health. She founded Childline in 1986 after a That's Life! episode on child abuse prompted an overwhelming public response — BACP's tribute notes more than six million children and young people have been helped by it since — and later founded The Silver Line, a helpline for older people experiencing loneliness. BACP's chief executive, Dr Phil James, called the organisation "proud of her connection with BACP and the counselling profession."

🔬 Research worth knowing about — a thin week

On this newsletter's standing priority: do pregnancy-era interpersonal therapy benefits for depression last through the first postpartum year? A Psychiatric Times write-up of the "Care Project" trial (Hankin, Davis and colleagues, Journal of Mood and Anxiety Disorders) reports that pregnant people with elevated depressive symptoms, identified through routine obstetric screening, were randomised to either brief interpersonal psychotherapy (IPT) or enhanced usual care. The IPT group's depressive symptoms and rates of major depressive disorder fell faster during pregnancy than the comparison group's — and, unlike some short-term interventions, the gain did not fade afterwards: the researchers report the benefit held up through the first full year postpartum, in both symptom severity and diagnosis rates. Worth flagging for anyone assuming a pregnancy-only intervention's effects wash out once the baby arrives.
