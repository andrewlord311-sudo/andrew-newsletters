🏥 A new NHS mental health crisis unit is coming to Colchester Hospital

Colchester Hospital is getting a dedicated Mental Health Urgent Care Unit, due to open this summer, redeveloped from existing buildings on site. It's one of two new such units across Essex — the other at Princess Alexandra Hospital in Harlow — built and run by Essex Partnership University NHS Foundation Trust (EPUT), giving people in mental health crisis a calmer space for rapid assessment away from a busy general A&E, with specialist staff working out whether the right next step is admission or support at home via the community mental health team.

Worth knowing This is crisis/urgent care rather than talking therapy or counselling directly — it won't replace a psychodynamic or CBT referral — but it lands in the same funding wave as the Westminster government's 6 August announcement of 159 new NHS mental health facilities nationally (100 walk-in community mental health centres plus 59 mental health emergency departments), which both BACP and UKCP responded to this month — see the industry-bodies section below.

New this week: does an "embodied" psychodynamic approach help with depression? A systematic review and meta-analysis in Frontiers in Psychology, by Heider, Sulz and Franzen, pooled randomised controlled trials of psychodynamic treatments that take an "embodied" approach to depression — working with a patient's subjective, felt experience of their own body, rather than relying purely on verbal insight — against control conditions and other established ("bona fide") therapies. The finding, in plain terms: this style of psychodynamic work looks potentially effective for depression, broadly in line with talking therapy generally, but the authors are candid that the field hasn't yet settled what an "embodied" technique consistently consists of in practice, and call for more conceptual groundwork before firmer claims can be made. Worth flagging as a direction of travel rather than a verdict.
