🏆 BACP Member Awards go to a perinatal mental health charity and its founder

BACP has published the winners of its 2026 Member Awards, presented at its Member Event in London on 10 October. Two of the results sit squarely on this newsletter's standing priority: Dr Miriam Donaghy won Distinguished Contributions to Counselling and Psychotherapy, and the charity she founded and leads, MumsAid, won Excellence in Service Delivery (Voluntary and Community). MumsAid, based in Greenwich, describes itself as an "award-winning specialist perinatal mental health charity" — it offers free counselling for pregnant women and new mothers through local children's centres, a dedicated YoungMumsAid service for mothers aged 21 or under, an out-of-hours text line, and birth-trauma therapy, taking referrals from NHS perinatal mental health and IAPT services as well as self-referrals. The same awards round also recognised Christopher Butler (Lifetime Achievement) and Silva Neves (Distinguished Contributions to Diversity, Equity, Inclusion and Belonging), among others, all named on BACP's own awards page linked above.

🔬 Research worth knowing about — a thin week

How does short-term psychodynamic therapy for social anxiety actually compare with other treatments? A new meta-analysis in BMC Psychology (Norén, Lindqvist, Lilliengren and Mechler) pooled 11 randomised controlled trials, 1,167 participants in total, of short-term psychodynamic psychotherapy (STPP) for social anxiety disorder. Against waitlist controls, STPP showed a large benefit. Against other active treatments — mostly CBT, which dominates NHS referral pathways for anxiety — the difference was not statistically significant, meaning STPP performed roughly as well rather than better or worse. Where follow-up data existed, the gains held up over time and in some cases kept improving. The authors are careful to call for more head-to-head trials and longer follow-up before drawing firm conclusions, but the headline is useful on its own: for social anxiety specifically, psychodynamic therapy has real trial evidence behind it as an alternative to CBT, not just a historical one.
